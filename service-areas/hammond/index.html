Warning signs

When Should a Hammond Home or Business Call an Electrician?

Call when your wiring starts sending signals. Shocks or a tingling feeling from a switch, lights that dim, and breakers that trip again and again often point to faulty or overloaded wiring. A burning or smoky smell is a reason to stop using that circuit and get a licensed electrician to check it.

  • A shock or tingle when you touch a switch or appliance
  • Lights that dim or flicker without a loose bulb
  • Breakers that trip or fuses that blow again and again
  • A burning or smoky smell near outlets or switches
  • An outlet with no power while its breaker is on
  • A GFCI outlet that will not reset

Frequently Asked Questions About Electrical Work in Hammond

More Answers

Why won't my GFCI outlet reset?

A GFCI outlet that will not reset usually means it is detecting a ground fault somewhere on the circuit and staying off to protect you. Unplug everything on it and press reset firmly. If it trips again right away, the outlet may be faulty and a licensed electrician should check it.

What are the warning signs of bad wiring in a house?

The common signs are shocks or a tingling feeling when you touch a switch or appliance, lights that dim or flicker, and breakers that trip often. A burning or smoky smell is another. A licensed electrician can assess the existing wiring and tell you whether a repair or rewiring fits.

How do I check permit rules for electrical work in Hammond?

Start with the City of Hammond Building department, which takes building permit applications inside city limits. The city also keeps a contractor registration form. Rules can differ inside and outside city limits in Tangipahoa Parish, so confirm which office covers your address before electrical work begins.

Why is an outlet dead when the breaker is on?

A dead outlet often traces back to a tripped GFCI outlet elsewhere on the same circuit. Check and reset every GFCI outlet in the home first. If power still does not return, the outlet may have a failed part or a loose wire inside the box, which needs a licensed electrician.
