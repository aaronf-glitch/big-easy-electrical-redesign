Warning signs

How Do You Know a LaPlace Home Needs Electrical Repairs?

Your home usually tells you before something fails, through breakers that trip often, flickering lights, dead outlets and wiring that cannot keep up. Older panels and wiring were not built for many modern appliances. A licensed electrician can find the cause and recommend a repair, a panel upgrade or rewiring.

  • Breakers that trip when several appliances run
  • Lights that flicker when an appliance turns on
  • Outlets that stop working or spark
  • Fuses that blow more than once
  • Plans to add new appliances to an older panel
  • Signs of outdated or damaged wiring

About LaPlace

What the community of LaPlace is, according to St. John the Baptist Parish government's website.

East Bank Community

St. John the Baptist Parish lists LaPlace among its east bank communities, along with Reserve, Lions, Garyville and Mt. Airy. The parish describes each of them as a thriving, bustling community.

Home of Parish Offices

St. John the Baptist Parish government lists its address as 1811 West Airline Highway in LaPlace. The parish seat itself was moved to Edgard in 1848, according to the parish history page.

Andouille Capital

St. John the Baptist Parish says LaPlace has been designated the "Andouille Capital of the World." Andouille, a gumbo sausage, was traditionally made in winter, when hogs were butchered at "La boucherie."

Frequently Asked Questions About LaPlace Electrical Work

More Answers

Do I need a permit for electrical work in LaPlace?

Often, yes. The St. John the Baptist Parish residential permit application lists electrical as one of its permit types and asks for the contractor's license number. The parish handles those applications from its office in LaPlace, so check with that office about your specific job.

What age should a house be rewired?

No single age applies to every home, but many older homes have outdated systems that cannot handle modern appliances. That can overload circuits and raise the risk of electrical fires or shocks. Shocks, flickering lights and frequent breaker trips are signs to have a licensed electrician assess the wiring.

Can a whole house be rewired without removing drywall?

It depends on the house, since rewiring often means reaching wiring hidden behind walls and ceilings. We start with an assessment of the existing wiring, then plan the scope of work and timeline before any wires come out. We explain how we will reach the wiring when we give you the quote.
